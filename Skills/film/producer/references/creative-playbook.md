# Creative Playbook — the concepts underneath the craft

The reference library isn't a collection of well-specified shots. It's a collection of *ideas* that happen to be well specified. This file is the idea layer: the devices that make the approved work interesting, and the seven production modes to pick from at the top of a brief.

---

## Part 1 — The concept engines

### 1. Contrast is the engine

Nearly every strong piece in the library runs on a single opposition, held in one frame or across one cut.

| Contrast | How it shows up |
|---|---|
| Cold → warm | A woman rushes through a cyan Barcelona, climbs a staircase, and the grade flips to golden hour on her body mid-climb. The colour change *is* the story: stress → belonging. |
| Frozen → moving | Time stops on a street; only the camera orbits and only the wind in his jacket moves. Parallax becomes the subject. |
| Still subject → violent world | A woman stands almost motionless while enormous luminous clouds billow and fold behind her. |
| Serious frame → absurd line | Epic twilight battlefield, anamorphic, crushed blacks, a dying man claws out of the earth to rasp a joke. |
| Mundane → impossible | An old man in a car wash calmly puts on paper 3D glasses and watches the water like a movie. |
| Ordinary object → extraordinary treatment | A leather shoe or a t-shirt shot with the discipline of a car commercial. |

**Working method: name the two poles before writing a single spec line.** If a brief has only one pole, it doesn't have an idea yet.

### 2. Pick the moment *before* the action

> `Focus on human concentration immediately before extreme action.`

A boxer pulling the last wrap tight. A driver securing a glove. A visor coming down. A single breath at the edge of a platform. The library's strongest montage doesn't show the fight, the lap, the jump — it shows the ten seconds before each one. Anticipation is more watchable than event, and it is also far easier to generate cleanly.

### 3. The fake-out

Set up a read, hold it for a beat, then reveal something else. `She raises a heavy chrome pistol-shaped lighter beside her cheek. For one silent beat it reads as a weapon. Her thumb presses the side switch, a small yellow wick flame reveals the mechanism.` The instruction to hold the misread for a beat is the whole device — without `for one silent beat it reads as`, there's no fake-out, just a lighter.

### 4. Deadpan absurdity

Raise the absurdity, keep every performance completely straight. `Throughout, his expression remains serious, without any emotional fluctuation.` An alpaca buckled into a back seat that thrashes, then stills, spits a piece of hay, and stares back innocently. Six women in motorcycle helmets calmly playing cards in an artificial windstorm. The comedy lives entirely in the gap between event and reaction — so direct the reaction to be *nothing*.

### 5. Match cut as narrative glue

The most efficient structural device available. Chain a shared gesture or shape across unrelated worlds and they become one sentence: hand-wrap tightening → racing glove tightening → visor lowering → visor lowering → inhale → inhale → jump downward → ski drop downward → snow fills the lens white. Seven sports, one film, ten seconds.

Variants: **stride-matched cut** (location changes on a footfall, walk continues — six towns become one journey); **flash cut** (a shutter burst separates poses, turning a montage into a photo shoot); **motivated wipe** (a flag, a pillar, a passing person crosses the lens and the shot has changed).

### 6. The corridor / doors device

A tracking shot down a corridor where each opening door reveals a self-contained absurdist tableau, and the final door opens onto somewhere impossible. An anthology engine: it lets six unrelated ideas coexist inside one continuous camera move, with one recurring character stitching them. Reusable structure — swap corridor for windows, market stalls, train compartments, screens.

### 7. Engineered virality (social/UGC)

The reel-style prompts share an exact recipe:

1. A **physical event with visible cause and effect** — something tears, spills, breaks, arrives.
2. A **spoken line with the CTA fused in** — `"My pants did not survive that kick. Comment 'part 2' if you want it."` The call-to-action lives inside the performance, not as an overlay.
3. **Camera locked, phone-real** — static, no zoom, no push-in, stated three times.
4. **Natural sound only, no music.**
5. **An explicit anti-AI thesis** — `looks exactly like a casual real Instagram reel filmed on iPhone`, `very realistic and natural reaction, not AI looking`.
6. **Duration 10–12s**, single continuous shot.

For candid two-person energy: overlapping dialogue where the lines interrupt each other, plus physical emotion symptoms (`grips the sides laughing hysterically, eyes wide`), plus `camera shaking as it follows them, motion blur, candid chaotic joyful energy, genuine unscripted laughter`.

### 8. Product-as-hero, three doctrines

- **Studio reverence** (luxury goods): dark minimal set, one soft key plus rim, macro → silhouette → craftsmanship detail → dynamic hero → final locked hero. The product never spins, never floats, never orbits endlessly. Light and camera move; the product does not.
- **Documentary discovery** (apparel, lifestyle): `it should feel like a documentary filmmaker noticed the shirt inside the crowd and began following it instinctively.` Real location, real crowd, handheld, motivated cuts, subject behaves like a person rather than a model. Fidelity enforced by percentage of screen time rather than by staging.
- **Anti-cinematic credibility** (automotive, especially night): amateur phone footage, no grade, true dynamic range, Doppler audio, no other cars in frame, no plates. The absence of production value *is* the production value.

### 9. Anti-AI hygiene: name the cliché to escape it

Every generator has a default shape for every genre, and the default is the cliché. The library's method is to name it and ban it:

- Product: `NOT a generic AI product video. No 360-degree spin. No floating product. No endless orbit. No excessive particles.`
- Camera: `NO wide-angle, no fisheye, no barrel distortion. NO overhead, no crane, no drone, no god's-eye.`
- Transitions: `Hard cuts only. No dissolves, no fades, no whip transitions. Never liquid morphing.`
- Skin/faces: `no plastic skin, no beauty filter, no perfect symmetry, no over-sharpening.`
- Performance: `no heroic posing, no exaggerated slow motion, no fashion runway walking, no robotic body motion, no smiling at camera.`

If a piece is going to be judged on looking real, the negative list is not optional garnish — it is half the prompt's work.

### 10. Lock everything, change one variable

The cleanest continuity strategy in the library. A mascot walks left-to-right through six countries with `the same street-level camera height, lateral viewing angle, subject size, screen position, walking speed, lens compression, soft natural daylight, muted warm colour grade and cinematic depth of field across every location.` Only the location changes. A muscle car runs one tunnel from five camera positions with `identical car, paint, wheels, stripes and ride height across all five cuts, same tunnel, same lane, same direction of travel, same time of night, same lighting state.` Only the camera changes.

**Ask of every multi-shot piece: what is the one variable? Then lock the rest, out loud.**

### 11. Restraint as a quality signal

The library's premium work is quiet. `Moderate movement to maximise face stability.` `The camera moves only slightly.` `Do not rotate the product.` `Extremely subtle push-in.` `Expressions restrained.` `Near silence.` Frantic camera movement is the amateur tell and also the generation-failure tell. When something must be flawless — a face, a logo, a product — spend the motion budget on the world around it, not on it.

### 12. Reference-by-name as compression

`Live action cinematic. Tarkovsky style.` Four words carrying pace, colour, framing and duration. Similarly: `1970s European fashion editorial mixed with contemporary absurdist cinema`, `desert-noir heartbreak fantasy`, `Brazilian football documentary photography`. A well-chosen two-reference collision does more than a paragraph. Use it as the STYLE block's opening line, then specify underneath it — the name sets the target, the spec keeps the model from missing.

### 13. Sound designs the edit

Not decoration: structure. The main beat drop lands exactly when the product reaches full rotation. A shutter click fires on every cut. Audio cuts to near-silence in the final half-second. Crowd sound grows as the subject leaves the corridor. When you know where the audio hits, you know where the cuts are — write the audio and the cut sheet together.

## Part 2 — The seven production modes

Pick the mode at the top of every brief. It determines which blocks are required, the lens and light defaults, the duration, and the audio policy. Getting the mode right is 80% of the work; the mode carries most of the decisions with it.

### Mode A — Spec-Stack Commercial
*Premium product, vehicle, or brand film. The full architecture.*

- **Required blocks:** thesis, format, references, subject, location map, cut sheet, optics, camera, action, physics, lighting, colour, surface, audio, style, output, positive locks, negative prompt.
- **Optics:** 50mm floor; 85/100/135mm for compression; hub-to-eye height only; ban drone and wide-angle.
- **Light:** practical only, no fill, hard falloff, haze, Kelvin values.
- **Colour:** 60:30:10, product owns the only saturation.
- **Cuts:** hard, exact durations, motion on frame one.
- **Audio:** environmental SFX only, no music.
- **Length:** 600–1,400 words. **Duration:** 10–25s.
- **Examples in library:** tunnel muscle car, leather shoe, Jordan 1, Campari Barcelona.

### Mode B — Anti-Cinematic Credibility
*UGC, reels, dashcam, found footage, night automotive. The goal is that nobody suspects production.*

- **Required blocks:** thesis (explicitly anti-cinematic), format, references, subject, camera (locked, stated redundantly), action beats, dialogue with CTA if social, audio (natural only), style (amateur phone stack), constraints.
- **Optics:** phone-equivalent, static, natural micro-shake only.
- **Light:** available light, no grade, true-to-source dynamic range.
- **Audio:** diegetic only, never music.
- **Length:** 120–250 words. **Duration:** 8–12s, single continuous shot.
- **Examples:** karate-gi reel, shopping-cart night, GT3 dashcam pass.

### Mode C — Live the Still
*Image-to-video. A strong frame exists; add life without breaking it.*

- **Required blocks:** camera (near-locked or extremely subtle push-in), micro-behaviour list, environmental motion, preserve-identity clause, negative prompt (no talking, no sudden zoom, no distortion).
- **The formula:** subject does 3–5 micro-behaviours (blink, weight shift, breath, hair in breeze, slow head tilt) while the *environment* carries the real motion (clouds folding, mist drifting, dust in the beam, light flickering).
- **Length:** 10–150 words. Shorter than instinct suggests.
- **Examples:** pink clouds portrait, `slow camera movement. bring the subject & environment to life`, sky-fall, the looking-up-at-buildings beat.

### Mode D — Reference-Scoped Composite
*Multiple assets, each contributing a different channel: face from one, body from another, camera and atmosphere from a video, wardrobe from a third.*

- **Required blocks:** REFERENCE MANIFEST with `ONLY` scoping, anti-bleed bans per asset, identity-and-continuity paragraph, then normal shot spec.
- **The discipline:** unique token per asset; a declared channel per asset; an explicit ban on every unwanted element each asset carries.
- **Length:** 200–500 words. Most of it is the manifest.
- **Examples:** the face/body/video manifest prompt, the Vegas `element_tags` prompt, the AREN front/back product references.

### Mode E — Continuity Engine
*One variable changes; everything else is locked and the locks are written down.*

- **Required blocks:** the locked-parameter list (camera height, angle, subject size, screen position, speed, lens, grade, light), the changing variable per segment with timecodes, the transition device (stride-match / motivated / flash), positive locks, negative prompt covering drift.
- **Length:** 300–600 words. **Duration:** 10–25s.
- **Examples:** rabbit through six towns, five-angle tunnel run, ten locked editorial poses, five-setup flash montage.

### Mode F — Plate-Protected Replacement
*Swap or insert an element inside existing footage without touching anything else.*

- **Required blocks:** `replace only X` / `previously a green chroma-key surface`, an enumerated protect-list of everything in the background that must stay 100% identical, per-frame geometric match (position, size, angle, orientation), inherited camera path and grade, reflections and contact shadows, anti-flicker/anti-morph negatives, anti-CGI thesis.
- **Length:** 150–350 words.
- **Examples:** phone-screen football broadcast, W140 swap, BMW E34 swap, the truck livery preservation.

### Mode G — World Bible Anthology
*A recurring character and a fictional world across multiple pieces.*

- **Required blocks:** a `CONSISTENT WORLD AND CHARACTER LOCK` header carrying the place, palette list, era/style collision, film stock, recurring character's fixed description, aspect-ratio convention, and the permitted transition vocabulary. Then the episode's shot list.
- **Why:** written once, reused verbatim at the top of every episode. The palette and the character description are the franchise.
- **Length:** 150 words of bible + 200–400 per episode.
- **Example:** Porto Luma / Mara Vela.

## Part 3 — Working process

1. **Name the mode.** A–G. If two seem to apply, the deliverable decides: what does the client actually look at?
2. **Write the thesis sentence.** One line. Everything downstream must obey it.
3. **Name the two poles of the contrast.** No contrast, no idea — go back.
4. **Name the one variable** and list what stays locked.
5. **Map the references** with `ONLY` scoping and anti-bleed bans.
6. **Build the cut sheet with the audio** — hits, beats and cuts get written together.
7. **Fill the craft blocks with numbers**: focal length, height, Kelvin, cm of handheld drift, km/h of wind, seconds to one decimal.
8. **Write POSITIVE LOCKS** — everything you'd be angry about, stated as fact.
9. **Write the negative prompt** from the failure groups that apply, and name this genre's cliché explicitly.
10. **Re-read once, cutting only identity and atmosphere prose.** Never cut a number.
